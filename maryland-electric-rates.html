How electric choice works in Maryland

Maryland customers still receive delivery service from the utility, while the supplier controls the supply side of the bill. That sounds familiar across deregulated states, but Maryland stands out because supplier shopping usually makes the most sense after you identify the exact utility territory first.

In practical terms, Maryland shoppers should compare within the correct utility board rather than assuming a statewide rate list works the same way everywhere. That makes utility selection one of the most important first steps in the Maryland flow.

Maryland utility territories matter

BGE, Pepco, Delmarva Power, Potomac Edison, SMECO, and Choptank Electric Cooperative each shape the comparison context differently. Supplier availability, default service framing, and the way rates are presented can vary by territory, even when the shopper only thinks of the decision as “find a lower rate.”

That is why Maryland comparisons tend to feel cleaner when the customer starts from the utility shown on the bill instead of relying on ZIP code alone.

Common Maryland switching mistakes

Comparing across the wrong utility board

Maryland shoppers can waste time by reviewing a list that does not match the utility on the bill. Start with the correct territory first.

Looking only at the headline rate

Rate, term length, monthly fees, and cancellation terms all matter. A single low number can hide the real shape of the offer.

Never setting a follow-up reminder

A strong rate today still needs a future check-in. That is where alerts and plan tracking become useful.

Maryland electric rate FAQ

Can I compare Maryland rates without knowing my utility?

You can start researching, but a clean Maryland comparison usually depends on the exact utility territory shown on the bill.

Does switching suppliers change who delivers power?

No. Your Maryland utility still handles delivery and outages. Supplier choice only affects the supply side of the bill.

Is Maryland better as a one-time search or an ongoing habit?

Like most competitive markets, Maryland works better when shoppers compare again before a current plan grows stale.
