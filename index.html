Core product story

A website that funnels people into the strongest experience

This version is intentionally app-first. The site explains the value, builds trust, and moves people into the iPhone and Android apps where camera access, saved bills, notifications, and comparison history work best.

Bill scan that starts the process fast

The apps support photo uploads, document scans, PDFs, manual fixes, and local fallback parsing when needed.

Supplier comparison that feels less messy

Customers can review benchmark rates, shortlist eligible plans, and see estimated monthly or yearly savings.

Saved history, reminders, and follow-through

The mobile apps are the right place for bill history, contract timing, notifications, and repeated check-ins.

Cleaner trust story on the web

The site can carry the privacy, support, and market-coverage story while the apps handle the personalized workflow.

Why not make the whole thing web-first?

Because the app experience is already the sharpest part of the product.

A static marketing site is the fastest good move right now. It gets your story live on choosemyelectric.com, helps with search and credibility, and funnels people into the product that already knows how to handle bills, PDFs, saved history, and reminders.

Best next step after launch

Use this as phase one, not the ceiling.

Once this version is live, we can decide whether the next move is a richer web app, a logged-in customer portal, or just a sharper app-install funnel with better analytics.

FAQ

Short answers people will want before they install

What does Choose My Electric do?

It helps people upload a bill, review their current supplier and rate details, and compare other plan options faster.

What states are supported right now?

The current mobile flow is aimed at Pennsylvania, Texas, and Ohio residential users, including Ohio electric and gas use cases.

Do customers need to type everything in manually?

No. The main path is bill upload first, followed by a review screen where customers can fix anything that came in wrong.

Why send people to the apps instead of doing it all on the website?

The app experience already handles photo capture, PDFs, saved bill history, reminders, and repeat use much better than a quick static web build.

Ready to push traffic into the apps?
